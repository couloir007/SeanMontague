# Trail Guides voice guide

Applies to all copy for the Kingdom Trails and White Mountains sections of seanmontague.com. Sean's global writing preferences also apply and aren't repeated here.

## Tone

| Adjective | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| Candid | Says plainly when a trail is beyond the reader's level or conditions make it a bad call. |
| Reassuring | Lowers the overwhelm: one starting point, one route, a clear next step. |
| Practical | Leads with what the reader needs to decide or do. Scenery supports the decision. |
| Authoritative | Grounded in Sean's firsthand mapping and riding, not aggregated "best of" claims. |
| Friendly | Sounds like a local pointing you the right way, not a brochure. |
| Plain-spoken | Explains local trail names and terms at first use. |
| Encouraging | Makes a first visit feel doable at the reader's level. |

## Banned on this project

- "epic," "hidden gem," "bucket list," "must-do," "world-class"
- Exclamation points
- Rider or hiker slang without a definition ("shred," "send it," "gnar"). Precise terms such as "berm," "whoops," or "scramble" are fine when defined at first use.
- Hand-written numbers for distance, climb, descent, grade, or route time. Use TrailMapper figures or a [TM: ...] placeholder.

## Sentences and point of view

- Second person ("you") for route copy, addressed to the page's one target reader.
- First person (I, me, my) only for Sean's own firsthand observations.
- Short imperatives at decision points: "Stop at the top. Turn around."
- Longer sentences are fine for describing terrain, one idea per paragraph, 1 to 4 sentences each.

## On-voice example

"Cross to East Burke Sports. Lock your bike. Get in the river behind the shop." (Darling Hill Signature draft, April 2026)

Why: a specific place, a concrete action, local knowledge a visitor can't get elsewhere, no hype.

## Off-voice example

"Kingdom Trails is an epic, world-class destination with something for every rider!"

Why: two banned words and an exclamation point, no target reader, no place, nothing the reader can act on. It could come from any travel blog.

## Quality bar

- Great: a newcomer knows within 30 seconds whether this ride or hike fits them. Bad: the fit is buried under scenery.
- Great: reads like Sean's own field notes, cleaned up. Bad: could come from any travel blog.
- Great: one route, one clear recommendation. Bad: five alternatives with no opinion.
