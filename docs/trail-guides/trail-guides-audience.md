# Trail Guides audience

## The shared trait

Every reader is new to the area, whatever their skill. The site covers all levels; each page targets exactly one reader and names them in its first two sentences.

## Kingdom Trails: first-time visitor

**Segments (one per page):** beginner rider, intermediate rider, advanced rider, family with kids.

**One line:** A rider arriving in East Burke for the first time, facing a large network split into zones, with no idea where to start.

**Top three pain points**
1. Too many trails and zones. Existing guides dump a trail list instead of a first-day plan.
2. Can't tell which trails match their level.
3. Logistics: where to park, where to buy a pass, where a route starts and ends, what's open.

**What they want:** a decision-free first day at their level that ends somewhere good.

**Words they use** (from the April 2026 keyword research): "kingdom trails beginner guide," "first time visitor," "best routes," "trail map," "zones," "best time to ride," "eMTB rules."

**What confuses them:** zone names and boundaries, trail names, the pass system, eMTB access, how conditions vary with soil and drainage.

**Where they look now:** KT's own site, Trailforks, TripAdvisor, travel blogs (April 2026 gap analysis).

**What earns their trust:** firsthand specifics, honest "not for you if" statements, as-of dates on conditions, no ads.

**What makes them tune out:** listicles, hype, trail lists with no recommendation, stale information.

**Segment notes**
- Beginner: wants nothing scary, and wants to know where it gets harder and how to skip it.
- Intermediate: wants the classic KT experience, with clear warnings where a trail steps up.
- Advanced: wants the big objective and the logistics for a long, remote day (water, shuttle).
- Family: wants confidence-building, short bailouts, and a reward at the end.

## White Mountains: hiker new to the Whites

**Segments (one per page):** day hiker, backpacker.

**One line:** A hiker who may be experienced in the Southern Appalachians, the Rockies, or elsewhere, but hasn't hiked the White Mountains.

**Top three pain points**
1. Doesn't know the trail names or which trailhead to use.
2. Doesn't know how terrain and conditions here compare to what they're used to.
3. Logistics: parking, overnight options, what's open.

**What they want:** one recommended route at their level, plus what's different here and what to plan for.

**What earns their trust:** firsthand specifics, as-of dates, no hiking basics explained to experienced readers.

**What makes them tune out:** generic hiking advice, peak-bagging lists with no recommendation, hype.
